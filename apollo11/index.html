Powered descent

The last minutes before touchdown.

The lunar module Eagle came down with alarms on the guidance computer, a boulder field under the planned landing spot and fuel running low.

  1. "1202"

    Program alarm

    The guidance computer flagged it was overloaded. Mission Control judged it safe to keep going, and the descent continued through more 1202 and 1201 alarms.

  2. Manual

    Armstrong takes over

    The computer was steering toward a crater ringed with boulders. Armstrong flew Eagle by hand, past the field, looking for flat ground.

  3. "60 seconds"

    Fuel call

    Mission Control warned about how much descent fuel was left before the crew would have to land or abort.

  4. "30 seconds"

    Second fuel call

    Eagle was still settling toward the surface, kicking up a sheet of dust.

  5. "Contact light"

    Touchdown, 20:17 UTC

    A probe under a footpad touched the surface. Moments later: "Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."

  6. 02:56 UTC

    First step, 21 July

    "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." Aldrin followed about 20 minutes later.

Crew

Three astronauts. Two walked on the Moon.

Commander

Neil Armstrong

Flew Eagle to the surface by hand and was the first person to step onto the Moon.

Lunar module pilot

Buzz Aldrin

Called out speed and altitude during the descent, then joined Armstrong on the surface.

Command module pilot

Michael Collins

Stayed in lunar orbit aboard Columbia, alone, to bring the other two home.
